Backings

How it stays on.

Iron-on
A heat-activated film. Press it onto cotton or canvas. Skip the iron on leather garments. The heat is wrong for them.
Sew-on
Stitched in place. The hold for jackets, uniforms and anything that gets washed hard.
Hook-and-loop
Velcro, so the patch can come off and go back. The usual pair with PVC.
Adhesive
Peel-and-stick, for a surface you will not sew. Tell us what it is going on.
Pin
A pin back when the same patch needs to move between garments.

Borders

How the edge finishes.

Merrowed
A wrapped overlock edge. The usual finish on an embroidered patch, and it keeps the rim from fraying.
Hot-cut
A heat-sealed edge. Clean on woven patches and on embroidered shapes that should sit flat.
Laser-cut
A precise cut when the outline should follow the logo, not a rectangle.

The five finishes

Which patch, and why.

Send the logo and what it is going on. We'll recommend one finish and mock it up before anything is made.

  1. 01

    Embroidered

    Thread stitched through a twill ground, so the logo sits raised. This is the finish most caps, jackets and uniforms are asking for. A name, a crest or a short wordmark all belong here.

    We turn your artwork into a stitch plan, and that plan is what you approve in the mockup. If the lettering is tiny, woven is usually the cleaner read, and we'll say so.

  2. 02

    Woven

    A finer thread in a flat weave. Small type and thin lines stay sharp, and the patch sits thinner than embroidery, which helps on pockets, collars and labels.

    Send the logo as it is. If the detail is too fine for a stitch, the mockup will be woven instead.

  3. 03

    PVC

    A soft patch moulded in flexible rubber. It holds its shape and colour in the wet, which is why it lives on bags and outdoor kit. The outline can follow the logo.

    Hook-and-loop lets it come off. Iron-on, sew-on or adhesive when it should stay. We'll match the backing to the material.

  4. 04

    Chenille

    Looped yarn on a felt ground. The letter stands up and feels soft. It is the varsity finish: school letters, club numbers, retro merch on jackets and hoodies.

    Bold shapes read best. Fine lines belong on embroidery or woven, and the mockup will show which.

  5. 05

    Leather

    Leather or faux leather, debossed or printed. A quieter mark than bright thread, for hats, denim and bags.

    On a leather garment, sew-on or adhesive is the safer fix. A hot iron is a poor match for leather.

Keychains

The logo, off the garment. Hard enamel is polished level with the metal. Soft enamel sits in a recessed colour with a raised metal line. PVC and embroidered keychains are there when you want the patch itself on a ring.

Split rings, lobster clasps or carabiners. Tell us how it will be carried and we'll recommend the hardware with the quote.

02

Enamel pins

Small metal pins for lapels, lanyards and merch. Hard enamel is smooth. Soft enamel keeps the metal edge proud of the colour, so the design feels dimensional.

Send the artwork. You'll see the metal finish and the colour fills in the mockup before anything is made.

03

Stickers

Cut to your shape, for packaging, events and handouts. A sticker is the light version of the same mark, useful when a patch would be too much.

We'll confirm the material and the cut when we quote. Nothing is printed until you approve the mockup.

04

Medals

Custom medals for races, clubs and finish lines. The face can follow your logo, with a ribbon and a metal finish chosen on the quote.

Send the event name and the artwork. You'll see a digital mockup before any metal is made.

Which backing should I pick?

Iron-on for cotton and canvas. Sew-on when it has to survive washing. Hook-and-loop when it should come off, which is the usual PVC choice. Adhesive or a pin when you won't sew it. On leather garments, skip the iron.

Merrowed, hot-cut or laser-cut?

Merrowed is the wrapped edge on most embroidered patches. Hot-cut seals a woven or flat edge. Laser-cut follows a custom outline. We'll match the edge to the finish in the mockup.
